# Deutsch on free will and universal explainers

*If everything is computation, and computation is just rules unfolding deterministically, then where is the free will? Aren't my "choices" just predetermined cascades, dominoes pretending to deliberate? Spent a lot of time on this question, and Deutsch's answer turns out to be both clean and surprising — it does not require breaking determinism, invoking quantum randomness, or smuggling anything mystical in.*

---

## The setup that makes the panic feel inevitable

Start where Deutsch starts. The Church-Turing-Deutsch principle holds that every finitely realisable physical process can in principle be simulated by a universal computer. This makes the universe a computational substrate at the bottom — not by analogy, but by physical postulate. So a person, as a physical system inside that universe, is a computation too. There is no exit door.

If you are a computation, and computation is rule-following — same as dominoes, just more elaborate — then where is the *you* that decides? Aren't your choices fully determined by your prior state, the way the next domino's fall is fully determined by the current configuration?

Deutsch thinks this panic is confused, and the diagnosis uses exactly the structure of computation itself.

## The high-level layer is causally real

Recall the two-layer picture. Computation is the lock between a physical layer and an abstract layer. For dominoes computing an addition, the abstract layer (arithmetic) is something *we* impose from the outside — the dominoes do not know they are adding. The interpretation is in the head of an observer.

For a person, the abstract layer is intrinsic. You don't just have neurons firing; you have **reasons, knowledge, explanations**. And these aren't decorative labels on top of the underlying physics — they are the right level of description for what is actually happening.

Ask "why did that person pick up the cup?" The correct answer is "because they were thirsty." Not "because such-and-such ions crossed such-and-such membranes." Both descriptions are true. Both describe the same event. But the high-level one is the *explanation*; the low-level one is the *mechanism implementing it*. Reciting the physics does not explain the choice. It only describes the substrate the choice runs on.

The technical point: trying to predict human behaviour from neuron-level physics, even if you had perfect knowledge of every synapse, would in practice be hopeless because of the same kind of computational irreducibility that makes Rule 30 unpredictable from its rule. The neuron-level description and the reasons-level description are both real, both causally complete in their own terms, and the reasons-level is the one that actually compresses the behaviour.

This is substrate-independence applied to people. The same reasons could in principle have caused the same choice via silicon, different neurons, or any other sufficient substrate. **The reasons are the real thing.** That is the subject of the explanation; the physics is the carrier.

## People are universal explainers

This is Deutsch's most distinctive claim, from *The Beginning of Infinity*. Humans are not just *some* computation. We are a special kind. We can construct **explanations** of anything that admits explanation. Our reach is unbounded in the same shocking way Turing machines are universal: cross a low threshold, and the reach becomes effectively unlimited.

This is a stronger claim than "humans are smart." Many animals are clever. The specific thing universal explainers can do that no other system can is *create new explanations that did not exist before* — about anything, including things vastly removed from the agent's evolutionary fitness landscape. Quasars, prime numbers, the structure of DNA, the meaning of justice. There is no a priori limit to what a universal explainer can come to understand.

Deutsch argues that this universality is what makes humans the most computationally significant kind of thing in the known universe. Not raw IQ. Not memory. **The capacity to generate explanations without ceiling.**

## Free will is what universal explainers do

Free will, on Deutsch's view, is not "freedom from causation." Uncaused choices are just random. Randomness is not freedom — it is noise. A coin flip is not more free than a deliberation; it is less free, because nothing in the agent's reasons or values caused the outcome.

Free will is what you call the kind of agency where your behaviour is **caused by your explanations** — by your knowledge, your reasoning, your conjectures about what is good or true. The cause is real (your reasons). And those reasons are *you*. So the choice is determined *and* it is yours. No contradiction.

This dissolves the standard incompatibilist worry. The fear "if my actions are caused by my prior state, they aren't really mine" assumes a self that exists outside the prior state. But there is no such self. The prior state, including all your beliefs and explanations and values, *is* you. A choice caused by your reasons is by construction your choice.

The dominoes do not have free will not because they are determined (you are too) but because they have **no intrinsic high-level layer of their own**. Their next state is fully captured by the physics. Your next action is not — it requires the explanation-level for its description to even be correct. The explanation-level is where you live.

## The objection that has teeth

A sharper objection: even granting all this, isn't your *next* thought constrained by your current state? You can only reach a narrow set of next thoughts from where you are now. Your current state was built by your past — what you read, who you met, what you experienced. So your "free choice" is a path-dependent unspooling of your history. A slightly fancier domino chain.

This is the version of the worry that genuinely has teeth. Three responses, in order of depth.

**The defensive move.** "Infinite options" is incoherent to begin with. What would unconstrained options even mean? Options that don't flow from your knowledge, values, or history? You couldn't *choose* among them. Choice requires evaluation. Evaluation requires reasons. Reasons come from who you are. **A chooser with no constraints from their own nature is not free — they are a coin flip.** Being shaped by your own self is what makes a choice yours rather than nobody's.

**The offensive move.** Yes, your *next* thought is constrained — only certain transitions from your current state are reachable in one step. But the *trajectory* you can reach over enough steps is in principle unlimited. The same shape as a Turing machine: each step is fully determined by the current state, but the eventual reach covers everything computable. **Locally narrow. Globally universal.** The right picture is not "four options at each moment" but "a generative process whose trajectory has unbounded reach."

**The deepest reframe.** The four-options framing breaks because you do not pick from a menu. You generate it.

Look at how questions actually arise in a serious thinking session. The objection you formulate three minutes into a problem was not on any menu when you started. It came into being when you reflected on what you had been thinking. The act of thinking did not select from an option-space — it **enlarged** the option-space. Where there were four options a moment ago, the act of thinking has now made it five, and the fifth is the new question you just generated. The fifth option had no prior existence anywhere in the universe.

This is the Popperian-Deutschian insight that the four-options metaphor misses entirely: **agency is not selecting among given alternatives. It is creating new ones via conjecture.** Every act of real thought *grows* the space rather than just navigating a fixed one.

## Knowledge creation is genuinely unpredictable

The deepest seat of freedom, on the Deutsch-Popper view: **new knowledge cannot be predicted in advance**, because to predict it you would already have to have it.

If a hypothetical perfect predictor could compute, today, exactly what novel scientific theory you will conjure next year, then the predictor already has the theory — which means the theory is not new. The act of conjuring a genuinely new explanation is the kind of operation that has no shortcut.

This is computational irreducibility applied to minds. **You cannot shortcut a person.** Even in principle. To know what they will think, you have to *be* them — and at that point it is not prediction, it is just them thinking.

The dominoes do not have this property because they do not enlarge their own option-space. Their next state is fully implicit in their current state, both *logically* and *expressively*. Yours is logically implicit, sure. But **expressively, your next thought brings something into existence that was not catalogued before.** That asymmetry is everything.

## The compressed picture

> You are a computation. That does not diminish you. Computations have layers, and your layer — the layer of explanations and knowledge — is causally real. Your choices are caused by your reasons, and those reasons are *you*. Because you create new knowledge, your future is genuinely open even in a deterministic universe. Free will is what universal explainers do when they choose for reasons that are their own.

What is striking about this position is how clean it is. It rescues free will without invoking magic, without breaking physics, and without smuggling you outside the computational fabric of reality. You are inside the fabric — but you are a particularly interesting region of it. The region that explains. The region that conjures genuinely new options into existence by thinking. The region whose trajectory cannot be computed from outside without, in effect, *being* that region.

That is a stronger and stranger sense of freedom than the libertarian "uncaused will" picture, and it survives contact with determinism in a way that picture never did.
